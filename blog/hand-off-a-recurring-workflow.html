How-To Reviewed 4 October 2026

How do you hand a recurring workflow to an always-on agent without losing the sign-off?

An always-on agent keeps working after you close the chat. That is useful only when you can still say what it was allowed to do, what you checked, and what you would not let it send. This is for people who already run a recurring workflow. The 4 October brief is the product context. The method below is the control.

Pick a workflow worth handing off

Multiply how often the work happens by how easily a colleague can tell a good result from a bad one. Weekly and checkable beats rare and fuzzy. A Monday exception list qualifies. A one-off strategy memo does not.

Anthropic describes Claude Sonnet 5.5 as strongest at well-scoped everyday tasks, and sets the default effort to Medium in Claude Code and its apps. Scope the handoff the same way: one outcome, named inputs, a finish line. If you cannot state those in a paragraph, the workflow is not ready.

Write the brief before it runs

OpenAI’s release notes describe dots as always-on agents: you give one a goal, connect the apps it needs, and define what it can do on its own. It can bring results back for your review. That is the brief, not a vibe.

Write four fields and keep them where the run starts:

  • Goal. One outcome, and what “done” looks like.
  • Inputs. The files or systems it may read. Name them.
  • Allowed tools. Read, draft, calculate. Anything else is off.
  • Stop conditions. Missing source, a figure that is not in the inputs, or a next step that would email, pay, delete, or change access.

The same four fields are the agent brief in the prompt library. Paste that shape. Do not let the agent organise its own scope.

Give it the least access that still works

Start from the tools the brief allows, not from the tools the product offers. Read-only on named files is enough for a draft. Send, pay, delete, and access changes stay with a person.

Microsoft’s Work IQ post says governed actions present the proposed change, follow any required approval, and update source records within the user’s permissions. Copy that rule even when the product is ChatGPT or Claude: the agent proposes, a person with the right access commits. If your admin has not turned a preview on, do not invent a back door. Wait, or do the step yourself.

Review a sample, and keep the gate

Check every row for the first two runs. After that, check every row that would leave the team, and a sample of the rest. Write down the sample rule before the third run so you do not relax it on a busy Monday.

A vendor stop is not your gate. Google says its Gemini 4 Argon mitigations monitor the model’s chain of thought and actions and stop execution when necessary. That is Google’s control on a limited-access model. Yours is still the brief: if a source is missing, the draft stops and asks.

Keep a failure log

One line per miss: date, what the agent produced, what was wrong, what you changed in the brief or the permissions. The log is how you learn. It is also how you show that the handoff got tighter, not looser. Do not delete the early misses when the later weeks look clean.

Turn it into proof your manager can see

Towards day 30, and again by day 90, put five things in one page: the workflow, the brief, the permission list, the failure log, and one before-and-after of the deliverable. Say what a person still signs. The career path is built around that kind of proof. A manager can see the control. They do not have to trust a claim about hours saved.

Example Monday exception list

This example is invented so the shape is visible. Swap in your own files. Priya runs a weekly operations review. The job is a short list of items that need a decision, not a narrative.

Why this one. It happens every Monday, and each row can be checked against a named export.

Goal
A draft table of at most five exceptions that need a decision this week.
Inputs
The incident export, the invoice queue, and the renewal list. Nothing else.
Allowed tools
Read those three files and draft the table. Do not email, do not change a record, do not look outside the files.
Stop
A file is missing, a figure is not in the file, or the next step would notify someone or edit a system.

Permissions stay read-only on those three files. For the first two Mondays Priya checks every row. After that she checks every row that would leave the team, plus one in five of the rest, and she writes misses in the log. On day 30 the page for her manager is the brief, the permission list, three log lines, and the Monday table beside the old slide she used to build by hand. The sign-off is still hers.

Questions people ask

Which workflow should you hand off first?

One you already do on a schedule, where a wrong row is obvious against a source you can open. If the result needs taste or a decision only you can make, keep the decision and hand off the assembly.

What if the useful tool is also the dangerous one?

Leave it off the allowed list. Let the agent draft the change. You, or the approval the product already requires, apply it. A preview that is off by default stays off until an admin turns it on.

What belongs in the 30 to 90 day proof?

The brief, the permissions, the failure log, one before-and-after, and the name of the person who still signs. Skip any number you did not measure.

Model names move. If a 5.5 request fails in Claude Code, the model config docs say which alias your provider resolves, and which version of Claude Code that model needs.
