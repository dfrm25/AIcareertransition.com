Connectors and MCP: what changes when AI can reach your work apps

A year ago, you pasted context into a chat box. Now the major AI tools connect straight to your email, calendar, files, and business systems. That is the most useful change in workplace AI, and the one most people turn on without thinking about it.

This guide explains what connectors and MCP are in plain English, what each major tool can reach today, and the four questions to ask before you connect anything at work.

Connectors and MCP, in plain English

A connector is the product word. It is the switch that lets an AI tool search your Gmail, open a Drive file, or read a Salesforce record. Every vendor has a slightly different name: connectors in Claude, Connected Apps in Gemini, Work IQ and agents in Copilot.

MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is the plumbing many of them use. It is an open standard that lets an AI app talk to another system through one common plug, instead of a custom integration for every pair. You will not build one. You should know the name because it is how new tools get added at your company, and it is what IT will approve or block.

Three levels of access

Not every connector does the same thing. The risk goes up fast as you move down this table.

LevelWhat it meansWhat can go wrong
ReadSearch and summarize your mail, files, calendar.It quotes an old draft as if it were current.
WriteCreate files, draft emails, edit docs and sheets.It changes a shared file in a way nobody notices.
ActSend, schedule, update a record, buy something.Something leaves the building that you never read.

What the major tools can reach today

Claude. According to Anthropic’s help center, Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Drive connectors are available to all users. Separate Docs, Sheets, and Slides connectors, in beta, let Claude edit those files live in a pane next to the chat. On Team and Enterprise plans, an owner has to enable connectors before you can use them. Anthropic notes that Claude can only open and change files your Google account can already access.

Gemini. Google calls them Connected Apps, managed in the Gemini app under Settings, then Connected Apps. On October 23, Google made Gemini Notebook a Connected App, on by default for most users and off by default in the European Economic Area. Google also says the Gemini app now connects to more than 40 apps.

Microsoft 365 Copilot. Work IQ decides whether Copilot pulls your mail, files, chats, and meetings into an answer. Microsoft’s preview adds Dynamics 365 and Power Platform business data. Our Work IQ on/off guide shows when to leave it on and when it picks up junk.

Four questions before you connect anything

1. What can it see?

Connectors usually follow your existing permissions. That sounds safe until you remember everything you have access to: old shared drives, a reorg folder from two years ago, HR threads you were copied on. The AI can reach all of it.

2. Can it write or send?

Start with read-only where the tool allows it. Turn on writing once you have watched it handle your files for a while. Keep sending and scheduling behind an approval step.

3. Whose name is on the action?

If the AI edits a file or sends a message as you, it is your edit. Microsoft’s new Copilot Studio agent accounts are one exception: their actions are recorded as the agent’s. Know which kind you are using.

4. Who approved it?

Connecting a personal AI account to work email is the most common way people get into trouble. Use the tools your company approved, signed in with your work account.

Good first connected tasks (read-only)

These save real time and cannot break anything:

Find every email thread from this month about [PROJECT]. List the open decisions, who owns each one, and a link to the thread.
Look at my calendar for tomorrow. For each meeting, pull the latest related doc from Drive and give me three lines of prep. Tell me the date each doc was last edited.
Find the latest version of [DECK NAME]. If there are several, list them all with their last-edited dates and owners. Do not guess which one is final.

Notice the pattern: every prompt asks for links and dates. Connected AI is only as current as the files it finds, and it cannot tell a final version from a draft unless you make it show you.
