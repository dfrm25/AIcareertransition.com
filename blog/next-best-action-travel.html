Next Best Action in Travel: One Guest, One Move, One Proof

Next best action (NBA) is not “send everyone 10% off.” It is the single next move that is most likely to help this guest, right now — and that you can defend to a manager with evidence.

For: marketing, loyalty, guest ops, and CRM analysts who already have a job in travel or hospitality
Not: a vendor pitch, a dashboard tour, or “ask ChatGPT for ideas”

If you work in travel, you already sit on signals: abandoned search, loyalty tier, days since last trip, open complaint, browsing a sailing you cannot sell them yet. The job is not to dump those fields into a model. The job is to turn them into one ranked action, with a human gate, and save the artifact.

This piece walks a real travel NBA loop you can run this week on tools you already have (Excel/CSV export + ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Copilot). You stay in role. You own the send.

What “next best action” actually means

NBA is NBA is not
One guest (or one segment of one), one recommended move, one reason A blast calendar with a new subject line
Ranked against do-nothing and against a weak alternative “Personalize at scale” with no veto
Checked against inventory, policy, and tone before it leaves Letting the model invent a perk you cannot deliver
Something you can screenshot for a manager A black-box score with no story

In cruise, airline, hotel, OTA, and tour ops, the expensive mistake is the same: the model sounds confident while offering a cabin class that is sold out, a fare rule that does not apply, or a apology credit your brand never authorizes.

The guest: Maya (worked example)

Use a fake guest. Never paste real PII, passport numbers, or payment data into a public model.

Signals (sanitized):

  • Loyalty: mid-tier; last sailing 14 months ago
  • Last 7 days: 3 visits to a 7-night Alaska page; cart abandoned twice at cabin select
  • Support: one closed ticket 11 months ago about delayed baggage (resolved with a voucher already used)
  • Email: opens newsletters; never clicks “book”
  • Constraint: party of 2; prefers balcony; budget sensitivity unknown

Wrong NBA: “Send 15% off all Alaska.”
Better NBA candidates (model must rank, not invent inventory):

  1. Concierge outreach: hold two balcony options for 48 hours + callback slot
  2. Content nudge: compare Alaska vs similar Mediterranean sailing she also viewed (no discount)
  3. Win-back: loyalty points estimate toward a future sailing (no fake balance)
  4. Do nothing this week; watch for third abandon

Your human gate picks one. The model does not send mail.

The loop (sense → plan → act → verify → share)

1. Sense — export only what you need

Pull a redacted row or small CSV: loyalty tier, last trip month (not exact date if that is sensitive), pages viewed (product IDs), abandon count, open issues (category only). Strip names, emails, phones, booking references if your policy says so — use a synthetic ID like GUEST_A17.

2. Plan — force a ranked list with refusals

Paste the prompt below. Demand:

  • Top 3 actions with why this guest, why now
  • What evidence is missing
  • What the model must not invent (inventory, prices, policy)

3. Act — draft the artifact, not the blast

Ask for one of: (a) agent brief for the phone team, (b) email draft marked DRAFT, or (c) CRM task text. One channel.

4. Verify — human gate (non-negotiable)

Before anything leaves:

  • Inventory / fare: can we actually offer this?
  • Policy: is the perk real?
  • Tone: does this sound like your brand, not a coupon bot?
  • Privacy: any leftover PII?

5. Share / proof — save what you would show a manager

Keep three screenshots or a one-pager:

  1. Signals used (redacted)
  2. Ranked NBA list from the model
  3. Final human-edited send (or “chose do-nothing”) with your initials and date

That pack is stay-in-job proof. “I used AI” is not.

Prompt to paste (travel NBA)

You are a travel CRM analyst assistant. You do not send messages. You do not invent inventory, prices, loyalty balances, or brand policy. GUEST (synthetic): - ID: GUEST_A17 - Loyalty: mid-tier; last trip ~14 months ago - Last 7 days: 3 views of Alaska 7-night product; cabin-select abandon x2 - History: one resolved baggage delay ticket ~11 months ago (voucher already used) - Prefers balcony; party of 2 - Opens email; does not click Book TASK: 1) List 4 candidate next best actions including "do nothing this week." 2) Rank them 1–4 for likelihood of a qualified booking conversation in 14 days. Give a one-sentence reason each. 3) For rank #1 only: write a 120-word AGENT BRIEF for a human phone agent (not an email blast). Include: goal, talking points, what not to promise, and one question to ask. 4) Add a VERIFY checklist of 5 yes/no checks a human must pass before any outreach. 5) If a fact is missing, write UNKNOWN. Never invent cabin availability or discounts. OUTPUT FORMAT: - Ranked actions (table) - Agent brief - Verify checklist - Assumptions / UNKNOWN

Swap Alaska for whatever product your export actually shows. Keep the refusals.

Retail cousin (same skill, different shelf)

If you are in retail, the loop is identical: browse + abandon + loyalty + service history → one action (hold size, stylist chat, restock alert, do nothing) → human gate → proof pack. Do not paste real customer phones. The career skill is the same: rank, refuse invention, own the send.

Common failure modes

  1. Discount as default. Models love coupons. Inventory and relationship often beat price.
  2. Invented perks. “We’ll upgrade you” when upgrades are controlled by revenue management.
  3. PII paste. Ticket IDs, emails, and card fragments do not belong in a consumer chatbot. Use synthetic IDs.
  4. Segment cosplay. “Next best action for millennials” is not NBA. One guest or one tightly defined micro-segment.
  5. No do-nothing option. Sometimes watching is correct. Force the model to include it.

Turn this into a 30-day proof thread

Week 1: Run the prompt on three synthetic guests from patterns you see at work. Save the ranked tables.
Week 2: Take one live case with policy-approved redaction; produce one agent brief a teammate actually uses.
Week 3: Log outcomes (conversation started / booked / no response) — counts only, no guest names.
Week 4: One-page write-up: what the model got wrong, what you vetoed, what you would automate later.

That is the artifact. It beats a certificate on a slide.
