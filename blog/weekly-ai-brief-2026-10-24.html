This Week in AI

AI moved into your files. Check its work there.

For the past month, most of the big releases had one thing in common. AI stopped handing you text to paste and started editing the real file: the Word doc, the Google Sheet, the deck your team already shares. That saves the copy-paste step. It also removes the moment where you used to read everything before it landed.

This brief covers what shipped from late September through October 24, 2026, who can actually use it, and the checks that keep it safe at work. Every claim links to the vendor’s own page.

The short version

  • If your company is in Microsoft’s Frontier program, Word, Excel, and PowerPoint now open inside the Copilot app.
  • On any paid Claude plan, Claude can sit in a sidebar in Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides and edit in place.
  • Gemini in Google Sheets can answer questions against a whole BigQuery table, not just the rows you can see.
  • Agents that keep working while you are away arrived as previews: Claude Duet, Copilot Studio agent accounts, and a tasks mode in the Gemini app.
  • New models: Gemini 4 Argon, GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, and GPT-6 with Intelligent UI in ChatGPT.
  • Your AI chats are records. Google Vault can now export Gemini in Workspace conversations for legal review.

1. AI now edits the real file

Three companies shipped the same idea within two weeks.

Microsoft, October 9. Office in the Copilot app. You start in Chat or Cowork, ask for a document, and a real Word, Excel, or PowerPoint file opens beside the conversation. Files save to OneDrive and keep comments, sharing, and version history. In Excel, Copilot shows what it changed. Who gets it: Frontier customers only for now. Microsoft has not given a date for everyone else.

Anthropic, October 6. Claude for Google Workspace, a sidebar in Docs, Sheets, and Slides. It reads the file and whatever you have selected, then edits in place. In Sheets it writes formulas, builds pivot tables, and adds tabs. The default mode, “Ask before edits,” shows each change as a card you approve. “Accept all edits” applies changes without stopping. Who gets it: public beta on all paid Claude plans. On Team and Enterprise, an owner has to turn on the Docs, Sheets, and Slides connectors first.

OpenAI, October 20. ChatGPT Slides builds a deck from a chat, files, or an outline, lets teammates edit it together, and exports to PowerPoint, PDF, or Google Slides. Who gets it: eligible Pro, Business, and Enterprise users with Space access.

Also on October 8, Anthropic moved Claude Docs, Slides, and Design out of beta and onto every plan, including Free.

What changes for you

Before, AI gave you text and you pasted it in. That paste was a review step, even if nobody called it one. You read the paragraph, you noticed the wrong number, you fixed the tone. Now the edit lands directly in the file. If a formula gets replaced with a typed number, or a paragraph quietly loses its caveat, nobody reads it on the way in.

How to use it without getting burned

  • Keep approval on at first. In Claude, stay in “Ask before edits” until you have watched it handle that kind of file a few times. Save “Accept all edits” for drafts nobody else depends on.
  • Copy shared files before a big edit. Version history helps, but rolling back a team file after three other people edited it is a mess.
  • Ask for a change log. End every request with: “List every cell, slide, or paragraph you changed, with the old and new value.” Then check three of them yourself.
  • In spreadsheets, read the formula, not the number. Click two or three changed cells and look at the formula bar. A typed value that used to be a formula is the most common silent break. Our Excel mistakes guide has the full list.
  • Read the final version where your audience will. Open the doc in the normal app, not just the side panel, before you share it.

2. Spreadsheets can now query the whole database

Google, October 19. Gemini in Sheets can analyze BigQuery data in plain language. After you connect a table through Connected Sheets, Gemini’s answers run against the full dataset, not just what is visible in the sheet. Ask for a quarterly review and it shares its plan first, then builds a new tab with pivot tables and charts that refresh on a schedule. Who gets it: Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, and Google AI Pro and Ultra. Rollout started October 19 and can take up to 15 days. You also need permission to query that data.

Anthropic, October 8. Claude Dashboards connects to BigQuery, Snowflake, Databricks, Redshift, ClickHouse, or Salesforce and builds a dashboard that stays current. You can click any number to see the query behind it, and each chart shows when its data was last refreshed. Who gets it: beta on paid plans. On Enterprise, admins have to switch it on.

What changes for you

This fixes one of the biggest problems with AI in spreadsheets: it only saw what was in front of it. It creates a new one. The answer is now only as right as the definition behind it. “Total sales” might include refunds, internal test accounts, or a region that was reorganized in March. The AI will not know unless you tell it, and the chart will look just as confident either way.

Checks before you present a number

  • Open the query. Claude shows it when you click a number. With Gemini, ask: “Show me the query, the filters, and the date range you used.”
  • Ask for the row count. Then compare one total to a number you already trust, like last month’s reported figure.
  • Put the definition in the prompt. For example: “Revenue means net of refunds. Exclude accounts tagged internal.”
  • Ask the table owner one question. “Is there anything about this table I should know before I use it?” Thirty seconds, and it catches most traps.

Try this: pick one report you ask the data team for every month. Ask the question yourself, then compare your answer with their last version. If they match, you have a faster path. If they don’t, the gap shows you the definition you were missing.

3. Agents that keep working when you step away

Anthropic, October 21. Claude Duet is a separate app where Claude acts as a proactive agent. It comes to you with what needs attention, finishes tasks you hand it, keeps working while you are away, and checks with you before acting on your behalf. Anthropic is direct about the limits: it “still might misread a thread or miscalibrate what’s urgent,” and its background checks count against your plan’s usage. Who gets it: research preview by waitlist, starting with US Pro and Max subscribers.

Microsoft, October 15. Copilot Studio agent accounts give an agent its own sign-in inside Microsoft 365. People can @mention it in Teams, Outlook, and in comments in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint. Its actions are recorded as the agent’s, not yours. That matters when someone later asks who changed a file.

Google, October 13. The Gemini app added a toggle from chat to tasks. In tasks mode, Gemini can keep working when you are not at your computer, across your Google apps and more than 40 connected services.

What changes for you

You stop operating the tool and start managing it. Managing means two things: writing a clear brief, and checking the work. Most bad agent results trace back to a vague brief, not a weak model.

A brief that works:

Goal: [one sentence]
Done looks like: [the file, message, or list you expect back]
Sources you may use: [folders, channels, tables]
Never: email anyone outside the company, delete anything, change a shared file without asking
Ask me before: [anything that costs money or reaches a customer]
Report back with: what you did, what you skipped and why, anything you were unsure about

Do not hand an agent anything that sends a message to a customer, moves money, changes someone’s access, or cannot be undone. Not yet. Start with work where a wrong answer costs you ten minutes, not a client. The agent workflows guide walks through picking that first task.

4. Which new model for which job

Five model releases in four weeks. You do not need all of them. Match the model to the task, and only if it shows up in your picker.

If you need Try Where you’ll find it
Hard, multi-step work across many sources Gemini 4 Argon Gemini app on Google AI Pro and Ultra; AI Mode in Search
Complex work at a lower cost GPT-6.1 Sol ChatGPT Work and Codex on Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Edu. Not in the Chat tab yet.
Everyday questions with charts and visuals GPT-6 with Intelligent UI ChatGPT Chat tab, every tier. Sol on paid plans, Luna on Free and Go.
Polished documents, slides, spreadsheets Claude Sonnet 5.5 Claude apps, plus AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure
Lots of quick, repetitive tasks Claude Haiku 5.5 Claude platform and the major clouds. Mostly for teams building tools.

Two cautions. First, Google says Argon answers “with less hedging.” That is nicer to read. It also means fewer built-in warnings, so your own check matters more. Second, an Intelligent UI chart is only as good as the numbers it was given. A clean chart built on a wrong paste is still wrong.

Not sure which model you are on? The model-switch guide shows where each picker lives.

5. Your AI chats are work records

Google, October 22. Google Vault can now export Gemini in Workspace conversations from Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Meet, filtered by person and date, for eDiscovery.

OpenAI, October 20. Locked chats hide a conversation from your history behind a PIN or Face ID. OpenAI says your retention settings stay the same. It keeps a chat off a shared screen. It does not delete anything.

Simple rule: write work prompts as if your manager or legal team might read them later, because on a work account they can. Use the approved work tool for work. Keep anything your policy says to keep out, out.

The proof move for this week (30 minutes)

  1. Pick one file you update every week: a tracker, a status deck, a budget tab.
  2. Have the in-file AI make this week’s update, with approval on.
  3. Ask for the change log. Check three changes by hand. Write down anything it got wrong.
  4. Save four things: the prompt, the change log, what you fixed, and the minutes it saved.

That one page is something you can show a manager. It shows the tool, the task, your check, and the result. Do it three weeks in a row and you have a pattern, not a one-off.
